# Personas

Four people Hawk Shop is designed for. Use them to decide what a screen shows
first and what it can leave out. Each stays under 500 characters on purpose.

## 1. Design mentor (power user)

Coaches students from CAD to release. Works on a laptop beside Onshape. Wants
every part a project has released, with release warnings, missing data and
stalled cards surfaced so they can fix them fast, often in bulk: reassign,
re-project, move. Uses search, filters and grouping without thinking. Checks
progress on a phone between conversations but does real work at a desk.
Success: nothing reaches the shop with bad data, and each student knows what
to fix.

## 2. Design student

Designs parts in Onshape and releases them. Knows CAD and how parts get made,
so plain shop language is fine. Not a power user: opens the app to answer
"where are my parts, and does anything need me?" Wants their own releases at
a glance, warnings they caused, and what the shop asked of them. Often on a
laptop at the CAD table, sometimes on a phone. Success: releases clean parts
and never has to walk to the shop to find out a part's status.

## 3. Shop mentor (power user)

Runs the manufacturing shop and watches the Slack channel. Thinks in capacity:
how big the queue is, which machines are busy or idle, what is overdue, what
is blocked on design. Grooms the backlog and assigns parts to machines, often
from a phone on the shop floor; does heavier work on a laptop. Follows Slack
links straight to a part. Success: the right parts on the right machines, no
idle machine with work waiting, and no surprises before competition.

## 4. Machine operator (student)

Runs one machine for the day: a printer, the router, a saw. Mostly reads a
shared shop screen, sometimes a phone; hands busy. Wants "what's next on my
machine, and how?": part, material, quantity, model, the releaser's notes and
the machine's SOP. Claims a part, then marks it done, with one tap each.
Glances at Slack occasionally. Not a power user; shouldn't need filters to
find their work. Success: never idle, never the wrong part or count.
